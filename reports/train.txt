Investigation lag L: what the export can and cannot tell us
====================================================================================================
share of CRM claims with a blank (undecided) label, by month first submitted:
  2025-10: 3.4%, 2025-11: 2.8%, 2025-12: 2.9%, 2026-01: 3.3%, 2026-02: 2.4%, 2026-03: 2.9%, 2026-04: 3.4%, 2026-05: 3.4%, 2026-06: 2.3%
  claims first submitted 16-30 June 2026, the newest in the export: 2.8% blank
The blank share is about the same in every month (2.3% to 3.4%) and does not rise for the newest claims. If blanks were simply 'not investigated yet' they would pile up at the end. So the export cannot reveal how long an investigation takes, and L has to be assumed.
VALIDATION HEADLINE: L = 14 days. Also shown: L = 0 (optimistic: every outcome known at once) and L = 30 (pessimistic).
At L >= 30 the history component CANNOT be validated in Fold B: only 20 new-regime claims are decided on 1 June at L = 30 (below the 50 needed), so every history feature is switched off there.
The FINAL model (src/final.py) freezes history at 30 Jun 2026 with every outcome known by then (L = 0 at the cut-off): the test set runs to 30 Sep, so for claims from August on, all June outcomes would be decided even under a 30-day lag.

FINAL MODEL: a documented deviation from the pre-registered rule
====================================================================================================
The rule chose M0. The user chose instead M5f: log-odds = M3 log-odds + 1.0 x log(partner_rate_ratio). (Which base model M3 stands for is settled by the robustness variants below.)
Why (decided by the user after seeing the results above; this is a judgement, not a result of the rule):
  1. M0 cannot rank claims beyond the partners with a fraud record: it orders all other claims only by how many decided claims their partner has. 15 of its 40 June places were filled by random tie-break.
  2. The intervals do not separate the models, so the rule's choice of M0 is not evidence that M0 is better.
  3. On Fold B at L = 14, M5f has PR-AUC 0.413 against 0.347 for M0 and ROC-AUC 0.870 against 0.843, and finds 11 frauds in the top 40 against 13 (a difference inside the noise).
  4. A coefficient of 1.0 lies inside what the data suggests: learned value 0.84 at L = 14 (90% range 0.00 to 5.00, from 2 frauds) and 1.18 at L = 0 (0.72 to 1.63, from 10 frauds). Fixing it avoids estimating a number the data cannot pin down.
M0 stays in every table as the benchmark.
